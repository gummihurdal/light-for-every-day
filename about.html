Why This Works
Depression affects more than 300 million people. Most of them will never read a research paper about what helps, and when you are depressed you cannot concentrate on one anyway. This takes a library of research and turns it into one short, kind, concrete message a day.
How the Messages Are Made
  • A curated library of more than two hundred documents (peer-reviewed studies, WHO reports, clinical self-help manuals) was gathered by health professionals in the church.
  • Each document was read and summarised; its key findings were tied to one of twelve themes.
  • Each day's message was drafted from those findings with the help of AI writing tools, under strict rules: no invented quotes, no invented numbers, no medical advice beyond the evidence, never a word against professional treatment.
  • Every message is read by a person before it is published. Readers can mark a message helpful or not, and that feedback shapes revisions.
  • Every message lists the studies it draws on. You can open them below.

The studies are copyrighted by their publishers; we link to them rather than copy them. The CBT self-help modules "Back from the Bluez" are published free of charge by the Centre for Clinical Interventions, Western Australia.

What This Is Not
Not therapy, not a diagnosis, not a substitute for a doctor. If you have been low for more than two weeks, please see one. Everything you write in the app stays on your device; nothing you type is sent to us.

Who Made This
A project of the team behind Grace for Every Day, with the Inter-European Division of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. It grew out of a conversation about sharing small daily messages with people who are struggling.
