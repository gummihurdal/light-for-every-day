Help, now

If you are thinking about ending your life, or you are afraid you might hurt yourself, please contact someone right now. Crisis lines are free, confidential, and used to exactly this. You do not need the right words.

When to see a doctor

Make an appointment if, for more than two weeks, you have had low mood or lost interest in most things, and this is affecting your sleep, energy, appetite, concentration or daily life. Go sooner if you have thoughts of death or self-harm, if you cannot look after yourself, or if someone who knows you is worried.

You can say exactly this: "I think I might be depressed. For the past few weeks I have felt … and I am struggling with … I would like to talk about what help is available." You can show the self-check score. A family doctor is a good first door; they can refer you to a psychologist or psychiatrist.

What depression is not

It is not weakness, laziness, a character flaw, or a failure of faith. It is a common illness that affects around one person in five at some point in life, and it is one of the most treatable. Most people who get treatment improve. Believers get depressed too; the Bible is full of people who did.

If you are worried about someone

  • Ask directly and calmly: "Are you thinking about suicide?" Asking does not plant the idea; it opens a door.
  • Listen more than you talk. Do not argue, minimise, or rush to fix.
  • Stay with them, or make sure someone does. Help them call a line or reach a doctor; offer to go along.
  • Remove means if there is immediate danger, and call emergency services.
  • Keep showing up afterwards. Depression lasts longer than a crisis.
